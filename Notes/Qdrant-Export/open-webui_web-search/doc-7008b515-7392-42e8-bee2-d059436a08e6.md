Before we proceed to the analysis of the cylinder, let us first briefly re¬ 
view the historical relationship between the Assyrians and Anatolia. The 
fullest textual evidence comes from the tablets of the Old Assyrian mer¬ 
chant colonies. Texts were found in the last two of four levels excavated at 
the site of Kultepe in the plain of Kayseri. Assyrian merchants resident at 
Kultepe called the area of the site in which they lived Karum Kanes. The 
older of the two levels - Karum II - lasted from approximately 1920 to 
1840 B.C., 1 when the area was destroyed. A second Karum - phase I b - 
was established on the same site about fifty years later, in the time of the 
great Samsi-Adad I of Assyria, who dominated northern Mesopotamia, 
including Mari on the border of Syria, for thirty-three years after about 
1813 B.C. 2 K. R. Veenhof describes the partial integration of the merchants 
with the local population through occasional marriages with Anatolians. 3
