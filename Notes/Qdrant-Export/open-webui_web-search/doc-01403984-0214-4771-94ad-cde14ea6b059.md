During the past decade, new excavations at the site of Konar Sandal South 
in the Halil River valley in the Kerman province of southeastern Iran 
produced a large and important corpus of glyptic imagery that allows us to 
expand upon the limited corpus of securely provenanced seals from Tepe 
Yahya and Shahdad to construct a more detailed picture of the visual 
language of the region during the third millennium Bronze Age. What I 
will present here is the evidence from those excavations that demonstrates 
beyond a shadow of a doubt that the unprovenanced seals presciently 
identified by Porada are indeed products of southeastern Iran, and more 
specifically that they are fine exemplars of the glyptic art of the Bronze 
Age civilization of the Halil River Basin. Furthermore, it is through those
