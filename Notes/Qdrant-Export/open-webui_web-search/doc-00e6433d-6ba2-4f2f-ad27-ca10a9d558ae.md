Edith Porada’s 1982 contribution on the lapis lazuli seals deposited at Tod 
during the Middle Bronze Age, and their political implications, was 
paralleled by a landmark work in the same year, on the subject of well- 
traveled lapis lazuli seals discovered in a foreign royal context during the 
Late Bronze Age. I refer to her monumental monograph on the cylinder 
seals found—along with lapis lazuli and agate beads, unworked lapis 
cylinders, as well as ivories and Aegean stamp seals—at Mycenaean 
Thebes. 10 The “treasure” was discovered in a building near the center of the 
Theban citadel, probably a jewelry workshop that was associated with the 
palace known as the Kadmeion. Porada’s comprehensive study will always 
have special meaning for those fortunate students, the author among them, 
who were privileged to participate in a seminar at the Pierpont Morgan 
Library, which turned out to be a master class in advance of her publication
