mained without influence upon monumental art in Babylonia. The few monuments of 
Neo-Bahylonian art which are known of the period before the Neo-Babylonian empire, 
such as the boundary stones, continued the Kassite tradition. Even when relief sculpture 
was produced on a large scale in the great upsurge of artistic activity under Nabu-kudur- 
usur II, this sculpture was distinctively Babylonian in subject and technique. There are 
no scenes of battles and hunting, the most conspicuous themes of the Assyrian reliefs, 
represented on the walls of the procession street and of the Istar-gate in Babylon; and 
the technique in which these Babylonian reliefs were fashioned is characteristic of work 
in clay in contrast to the Assyrian technique which is equally characteristic of work in 
stone. Cf. also R. Koldewey, Das wiederstehende Babylon. Leipzig, 1933, p. 29.
