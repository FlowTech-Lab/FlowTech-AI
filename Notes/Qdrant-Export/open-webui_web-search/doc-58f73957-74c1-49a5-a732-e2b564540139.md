Such cylinder seals were used to authenticate vast numbers of miscella¬ 
neous records: from letters to litigations, from dockets to deeds of purchase 
which were produced by business and bureaucracy in ancient Mesopota¬ 
mia. The simple device of securing movable property by sealing all access 
to it was probably discovered as early as the fifth millennium B. C., if not 
earlier. The cylindrically shaped seal stone, however, which was usually 
perforated in order to be worn as a pendant on a bracelet or necklace, was 
an innovation made by the inhabitants of Mesopotamia sometime in the 
latter half of the fourth millennium B. C. From that time on such seals were 
worn in that country for almost three thousand [26] years, and the extent of 
Mesopotamian influence in neighboring regions can be measured by their 
use or disappearance.
