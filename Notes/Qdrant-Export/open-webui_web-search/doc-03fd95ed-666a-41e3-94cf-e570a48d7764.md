Two female figures who raise a dog and a lion in the manner usually in¬ 
terpreted as a gesture of triumph over an inimical creature are the main 
subject of the cylinder (Fig. 1 and PL XIII. 1). At their right is a lion¬ 
headed figure holding up a double axe. The two identical figures wear 
headgear which turns up at the ends in a manner resembling horns and was 
probably meant to indicate the horned miters of Near Eastern goddesses. 
They will therefore be so called from here on. In the upper field between 
them is a winged griffin seated with its head turned backwards.
