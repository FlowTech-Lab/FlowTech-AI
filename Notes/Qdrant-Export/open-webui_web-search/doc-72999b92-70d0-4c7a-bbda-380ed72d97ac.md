The representation of animals in the Nineveh cylinder is equally character¬ 
istic of Middle Assyrian style. Most distinctive is the alert posture of the 
dog, which differs from the Old Babylonian columnar representation of 
what may have been the same breed of mastiff. 23 An Old Babylonian clay 
plaque showing a seated dog (fig. 8-4) can be compared with a Middle 
Assyrian sealing from Tell al-Rimah, also with a seated dog (ill. 8-8), to 
illustrate the difference between the styles. The upright ear of the Middle 
Assyrian dog gives him a tense expression, which is enhanced by the out¬ 
line of his back, creating the impression of a natural pose. Moreover, both 
hindlegs are shown, imparting a slight sense of threedimensionality. This 
effect is not unique in Middle Assyrian glyptic 24 and is typical of the lim¬ 
ited naturalism of this art, which sets it off from that of the preceding and 
following periods.
