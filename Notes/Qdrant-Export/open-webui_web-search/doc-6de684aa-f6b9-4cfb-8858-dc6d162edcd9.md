Another cylinder of great interest for which a Syrian origin is tentatively 
suggested (Fig. 4) shows the statue of a single large animal on a platform, its 
body covered by a fleece with the tufts of hair arranged in several registers. 
The animal’s head is covered by what now seems like a flat cap with upturned 
brim but which was probably originally a horned miter, as convincingly sug¬ 
gested by Contenau (Tresor de Tod, p. 18, s. v , pi. XLII, 15227). The large 
size of the animal and the careful, naturalistic engraving reminds one of the 
style of the Akkad period, the iconography of which includes a monster with 
heavy fleece and horned miter, carrying three females on its back (Fig. g). The
