Next to the table on the right in PL XVIII:6 is a censer or offering stand. 
To the left less space was available between the table and the seated figure. 
Therefore the offering stand was made much thinner, but a flower seems to 
project from it toward the seated figure. 

In one example, Pl. XIX:1, seen in 1971 in the Hadjiprodromou Collec¬ 
tion, of Famagusta, only one seated figure is shown. A lion appears in a 
cage, above which crawls a serpent on its undulating body. Between the 
seated figure and the lion’s cage is a tall table on crossed legs. On its sur¬ 
face a bird stands on one leg, one might think that a stork is represented.
