In the Royal Palace G of Ebla, of the third quarter of the third millennium B.C. 
(Mardikh IIB1: ca. 2400-2300/2250 B.C.), a large amount of raw lapis lazuli 
was found. There are several pieces whose weights range from a few grams to 
nearly 600 grams, with a total of nearly 16 kg. The largest amount is of pieces 
yet to be worked, and in one case a regular groove with a square section has 
been noted, most probably caused by a working tool. Evidence of the working 
of lapis lazuli at Ebla is offered by the actual finding of small objects, and also 
microliths that could be used as borers or scrapers. The raw lapis lazuli was 
found allover the building, but most came from the inner court (L. 2913 of the 
Administrative Quarter, where it probably fell from the upper storey, and in the 
southern hall with columns (L. 2866). These pieces and all fragments or parts
