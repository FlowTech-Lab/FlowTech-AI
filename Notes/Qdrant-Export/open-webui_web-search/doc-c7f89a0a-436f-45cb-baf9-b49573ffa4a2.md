In 1939 we cleared away the prickly oak scrub and excavated the rock ledge 
below the north temple terrace on the north summit of the acropolis of 
Mycenae. 1 Here we found on the edge of the steep rock remains of a wall of 
largish blocks of limestone. These we believe to have been part of a Middle 
Helladic fortification wall running round the upper part of the acropolis 2 behind 
this and held up by the wall were the ruins of two rooms and a thick deposit of 
M. H. pottery. Over this lay another stratum in which L. H. IIIB pottery was 
found. Here we found the splendid ivory group of two women and a boy which 
has already been published. 3 Associated with it were a number of other objects, 
a male head in painted stucco, 4 a sword pommel in white stone, some 
ornaments of gold and ivory, a number of beads of stone, paste, and faience
