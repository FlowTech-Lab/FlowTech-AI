To give an example of erroneous terminology, the verb ecraser , ‘to 
crush,’ is used for the action in which a person places a foot on the neck of 
a feline, thereby breaking it rather than crushing it, as suggested by the 
description (p. 24: A. 1); illustration (p. 41: Fig. 1.100). This shows a com¬ 
plete lack of understanding of the action and its implication. Placing the 
foot on the neck of an enemy is amply documented in Assyro-Babylonian 
texts relating claims of victory. An example is the statement of the Assyri¬ 
an king Tukulti-Ninurta I (1243-1207 B.C.), who said of his victory over 
Kashtiliashu IV, “[I] trod with my feet upon his lordly neck as though it 
were a footstool.” (A. K. Grayson, Assyrian Royal Inscriptions , I, Wiesba¬ 
den, 1972, p. 108 # 716).
