A close association existed between the drinking vessel in the form of a 
stag head and the stag as a game animal, as shown by the relief representa¬ 
tion on the Schimmel vessel. There a stag lies with its limbs folded, a pos¬ 
ture that probably means that the animal is dead, surrounded by two spears, 
a quiver, and what S. Alp convincingly interprets as the sacred fleece. 33 
Presumably the stag was killed in the course of a divine or royal hunt. The 
same combination of hunting symbols appears with the head of a stag in the 
outer band of a Hittite stamp seal in the British Museum (figs. 8-6 and 8-7), 
to which H. Guterbock drew my attention and which S. Alp also associates
