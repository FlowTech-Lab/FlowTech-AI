The sealing on Plate 13A (height ca. 2.6 cm.) shows a figure wearing 
such a simplified garment together with the horned miter and the ribbon 
hanging down the back derived from the attire of the suppliant goddess, but 
the pointed lower part of the face may have been meant to indicate that the 
figure was bearded. It is also possible, however, that the pointed face, like 
the similarly shaped hands, was gouged out of the sealstone with the same 
type of instrument with which the cuneiform signs of the inscription were 
carved because the seal cutter may have been trained primarily as a scribe 
rather than as an engraver of figures. This is probable also in other cases, 
but not as obvious in the sealing on Plate 13B (height ca. 2.6 cm.) where 
the strong clear lines ofthe face are somewhat unusual, including the eye¬ 
ball indicated by a small sphere in a manner not found in earlier Old Baby¬ 
lonian representations of human faces in which the eyeball was not 
stressed.
